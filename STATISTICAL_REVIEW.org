* Assessment and evidence boundary

Evidence file names and relative paths in this report refer to the course
audit directory =audits/gof-r-package-polish-2026-10-29/statistical-review/=.
This repository copy preserves the method assessment; the separate execution
manifest links it to the delivered source revision and package archive.

A final installed-candidate replay of gof 1.1.0 is recorded in the last
section. All 13 focused post-repair checks pass. The baseline findings below
are retained as provenance; they are not a claim that those reproduced
failures remain present in the repaired candidate.

The independent review found substantive defects in the previously
qualified numerical baseline: nonregular GLM fits can receive ordinary
p-values, moving-window statistics omit exit events and part of their
mathematical domain, and score-constrained ordinal processes can turn numerical
error into a significant test. It also found an insufficient contract for arbitrary
parameter-dependent ordering callbacks and an important loss of ordinal
distribution information under the default category-sum aggregation.

These findings demonstrate why software tests and a few successful null
calibration designs do not establish that every advertised statistical path
implements its intended method. Conversely, an explicitly limited feature
set is not itself a statistical error when the implemented test and its
assumptions are clear. This assessment distinguishes those cases.

The executable probes use the isolated installed gof 1.0.5 from the preceding
qualification, at commit =7fc29b89cc729c5e26ac79fda5ca4d171b1a6723=.
=baseline-source/= and =baseline-manifest.json= freeze the mathematical
source and its hashes. The baseline review ran alongside the version 1.1.0 maintenance work;
findings below describe that frozen baseline unless an
explicit post-repair addendum states otherwise. The reviewer did not edit

This is an evidence-based computational/statistical review, not an approval

* Findings requiring correction or a precise support contract

** F1, high: numerical convergence does not establish a regular estimator

The multiplier adjustment presupposes a valid first-order representation of
the estimated mean parameters. A =converged=TRUE= flag and finite machine
coefficients do not establish that the statistical estimator exists in the
interior of the mean parameter space.

=probes.R= and =probes.log= reproduce the following with 100 rows and 199
null draws:

- An intercept-only all-zero Bernoulli fit reports convergence and coefficient
  -26.56607. Its fitted probability is about 2.9e-12. Diagnostics ordered by
  an independent covariate return Sup/L2 p-values 0/0 and informative=TRUE.
- An intercept-only all-zero Poisson fit reports convergence and coefficient
  -27.30259. Its mean is about 1.39e-12; the same diagnostic returns 0/0.
- Completely separated logistic observations, with maxit=100, report convergence
  with slope 2230.8 and ordinary p-values around 0.337/0.899.

For all-zero Poisson data the log likelihood in an intercept beta is
-n exp(beta); it has its supremum only as beta tends to minus infinity. The
all-failure binomial likelihood has the same nonattainment property. The
small positive fitted means are stopping artifacts, not finite interior MLEs.
Their tiny constant residual drift can look extreme relative to the simulated
projected bridge. More Monte Carlo draws do not repair that problem.

=regularity-extensions.R= and its log show the same issue for Gaussian links:
negative observations with the log link converge numerically to a clipped
near-zero mean and return 0/0; an inverse-link fit with sample mean zero
returns a large finite eta although mean zero has no finite inverse-link eta.

Repair requirement: test the relevant existence/interiority conditions or
fail explicitly as numerically unresolved. Link-domain checks and separation
geometry must be distinguished. A coefficient-magnitude or fitted-p-value
threshold is not a proof. A constrained optimum can satisfy nearly zero
score with positive observed curvature while still lying on the mean boundary.

The maintenance work accepted this finding for implementation. The proposed LP
and numerical interior-root checks must be assessed link by link; ordinary
floating point interval bounds must not be called formal outward-rounded
certification. This report does not pre-approve unseen repaired code.

Gaussian exact mean fits are a different case. For ordinary least squares,
J=X'WX can remain nonsingular while every empirical residual and coefficient
influence contribution is zero. The resulting zero multiplier process is
well-defined as a degenerate mean-process limit. The separate Gaussian
variance MLE boundary does not require inventing a nonzero residual process
or rejecting an otherwise identified finite mean coefficient vector.

** F2, high for literal method equivalence: incomplete moving-window domain

The full window process changes when observations enter at X_i and leave at
X_i+b. It can remain nonzero until max(X)+b. The baseline evaluates only
observed covariate values, even with subset.max=Inf.

=window-domain.R= supplies a deterministic counterexample:

- Ordering values: 0,1,3; residuals: -8,10,-2; bandwidth: 2; no fitted parameters.
- Baseline grid: 0,1,3; complete event grid: 0,1,2,3,5.
- Baseline Sup: 4.618802; full Sup: 5.773503.
- Baseline L2: 4.898979; full L2: 7.659417.

Observed and simulated processes evaluated on the same incomplete grid can
still produce well-calibrated tests of that *different discretized statistic*.
This is why the earlier calibration suite did not establish full equality
with the stated moving-window method.

Repair requirement: evaluate the union of entry and exit events, use exact
upper/lower residual and derivative prefixes, and then apply any explicitly
requested evaluation cap to that event grid. Preserve the half-open interval
at exits and include its terminal support. Unrepresentable exit times or a
bandwidth smaller than coordinate resolution need an explicit numerical
handling policy, not silent erasure of the process.

The correction was assigned separately from this independent review.

** F3, high if arbitrary callback inference is advertised: omitted ordering term

Suppose Y=theta_0+theta_1 X+epsilon, with true theta_0=0, theta_1=1,
E(epsilon|X)=0 and conditional variance sigma^2. Define an ordering callback
x(theta)=X+c theta_0 Y. At the true parameter it equals X and therefore
satisfies E(epsilon|x(theta_true))=0.

Nevertheless, differentiating the population truncated-residual moment with
respect to theta_0 gives

  d E[(Y-theta_0-theta_1 X) I(x(theta)<=t)] / d theta_0
    = -F_X(t) - c sigma^2 f_X(t).

The implemented derivative of residuals alone supplies the first term and
misses the second. In a seeded 500-row example, the safe ordering gives
p-values 0.447/0.276, while c=100 in this callback gives 0/0. This single
example illustrates the missing term; it is not a rejection-rate experiment.

A sufficient contract is that the ordering and its parameter derivatives
are functions of data Z for which E(r(theta_true)|Z)=0, together with the
usual smoothness/equicontinuity conditions. Equivalently, the relevant
ordering-derivative term must be zero or must be included in the influence
expansion. Named fitted predictions based on exogenous covariates, and the
structurally safe Gaussian SEM projections, have such a justification.

Repair requirement: document this stronger condition on the advanced callback
interface, or restrict it to justified fixed/structurally safe orderings.
Checking numeric dimensions and centering at just one parameter value is
insufficient. No generic runtime validator can prove an arbitrary callback's
statistical assumptions from its name.

** F4, important estimand/scope issue: default ordinal sum discards information

For categories coded 1 through K,

  sum_{j=1}^{K-1} [I(Y<=j)-Pr(Y<=j|X)] = E(Y|X)-Y.

Thus the default aggregate=sum tests the conditional mean of that category
score. It is not a full check of the conditional categorical distribution or
the proportional-odds/probit specification.

=ordinal-scope.R= has two groups with probabilities (0.4,0.2,0.4) and
(0.1,0.8,0.1). Their ordinal-score means are both 2, while their CDFs differ.
The exact 2000-row construction gives default p-values 1/1, but the two
separate cutpoint processes give p-values 0/0 for both statistics.

A principled joint alternative uses ||W(t)||_2 across cutpoints before the
sup or L2 functional. The norm map is 1-Lipschitz, so the multivariate
conditional process convergence transfers by the continuous mapping theorem.
Sup is max_t ||W(t)||_2 and L2 is sqrt(integral sum_j W_j(t)^2 dt). The
existing ordinal sampler uses common subject multipliers and the full
nuisance-parameter influence adjustment across categories, as required.

The resulting norm process is nonnegative and not Gaussian or centered at
zero. Its plot and labels must say so. It is omnibus over cutpoints for a
*selected ordering*, not over every covariate or every model assumption.
Signed component displays remain useful for explaining the misspecification.
Version 1.1.0 adopts this joint default, retaining the
old score-sum method as an explicit compatibility option.

** F5, scope boundary: saved multi-variable columns are not joint draws

The native engine reuses a normal vector after reordering residuals and
influences separately for each ordering variable. Each marginal distribution
is correct, but the multiplier is then associated with sorted rank rather
than the same subject across variables.

=joint-coupling.R= uses complementary orderings X and -X with an intercept
projection. Complementary observed prefixes sum to approximately zero, as
they must. The corresponding saved simulation prefixes instead sum to
0.348, -0.091 and 1.137 in three draws, although each terminal process is
approximately zero. They cannot be parts of the same subject-level draw.

No existing one-variable p-value is invalidated by this observation.
However, the saved collection cannot support a new joint cross-variable
maximum statistic or simultaneous familywise bands. A future joint API must
index a common multiplier by subject through each ordering permutation, or
generate the full process once and reindex it. Marginal multiplicity methods
that do not require this simulated joint law are a separate option.

** F6, high: ordinal intercept-only processes are structurally uninformative

With no covariate columns, freely estimated ordinal cutpoints saturate the
marginal category CDF. The complete-grid cumulative residual process has
only a terminal point, constrained to zero by the fitted score equations.
Floating point stopping/derivative error is not lack of fit.

=ordinal-no-information.R= uses 150 observations and three categories. It
gives Sup/L2 p-values 0/1 for both the old score sum and a Euclidean norm
aggregation, from tiny observed residual totals versus still smaller null
numerical errors. =ordinal-current-before-noinfo.R= and its log additionally
freeze/replay the new norm default before the structural correction.

Repair requirement: recognize this model structure, set informative=FALSE
and p-values to one, and use the exact zero CDF process. Built-in sum/norm/
cutpoint transformations of zero are zero; a custom transformation should
be applied to that zero vector, which need not produce a zero constant.
The additional binary design is also reproduced in
=ordinal-binary-saturated.R= and its log: two groups of 100 observations,
with category counts (80,20) and (30,70), fit by an ordinal logit with an
intercept cutpoint and group slope. This saturates both group probabilities.
It nevertheless returns Sup/L2 p-values 0/0, with observed norm about 1e-7
and simulated norms about 1e-12. Thus an intercept-only guard by itself is
insufficient. For binary ordinal fits the augmented design [1,X] and link
score weight admit the same exact score-cell span argument as the GLM
adapter. A general symbolic detector for every reparameterized ordinal
model has not been established by these examples.

* Why the supported core construction is defensible

Write the truncated residual moment at an index t as a_i(t) r_i(beta).
Suppose beta_hat-beta_true = sum_i ic_i+o_p(n^-1/2), with ic_i the
coefficient *contributions* (usually order 1/n), and a valid centered
independent-unit influence representation. Let eta(t)=sum_i a_i(t) dr_i.
Here the representation refers to population influence values; the supplied
rows are their fitted estimates. Their unweighted sum can be zero by the
fitted score equations without implying zero uncertainty. Then the
first-order process has contributions

  n^-1/2 [a_i(t) r_i + eta(t)' ic_i].

The engine multiplies those two contributions by the SAME subject normal
within each ordering, and sums them. Its plus sign is correct because dr
is the derivative of the residual, which is negative for an ordinary mean
model. For a GLM, dr_i=-X_i mu'_i, and ic_i is the score contribution times
the inverse total information. This recovers the residual-process expansion.
For noncanonical links, expected Fisher and observed information are not
identical in finite samples, but have the same regular null limit because
the residual-dependent difference has conditional mean zero.

For precision-weighted rows, the score contribution contains the prior
weight while the raw residual remains one independent response-mean unit.
For independent binomial trials, expanding their indicator residuals and
using the original group fit's inverse information gives the appropriate
trial-level representation. This justification is stricter than a claim of
robustness to arbitrary within-group trial correlation; grouped overdispersion
needs group-level influence/multiplier handling.

For Gaussian SEMs, Cov(epsilon,V)=P(I-A)^-1 identifies structurally safe
manifest variables. Joint Gaussianity makes zero covariance independence.
With r=E(epsilon|all manifest data), iterated expectation gives E(r|Z)=0
for a safe Z. A smooth conditional prediction from this fixed safe set has
the required centering and ordering-derivative property. This supports the
named full-prediction extension under identified, regular Gaussian fits.
It does not establish a largest possible safe set under exact path cancellations,
or validate arbitrary nonlinear callbacks or non-Gaussian zero-covariance claims.

* Conditions that must accompany a sharable research interface

- State the actual target: conditional mean/functional form checks, and for
  ordinal joint mode, category-CDF checks along the chosen ordering. A large
  p-value is not proof that every aspect of the model is correct.
- State independent sampling units. Precision weights, trial counts, survey
  weights and cluster sampling are different statistical designs.
- State coefficient-influence normalization explicitly: raw scores or an
  O(1) influence function psi_i are not ic_i; usually ic_i=psi_i/n and their
  sum approximates the coefficient error.
- Explain mean-parameter domain and regularity failures. Exact Gaussian
  zero empirical influences and nonattainment of a finite mean estimator
  are different cases.
- Prespecify formal-test bandwidths, orderings and statistics. Searching for
  the smallest p-value over residual-selected bandwidths/grids or many
  covariates requires a selection/multiplicity procedure not supplied here.
- Distinguish fixed-grid approximations from the complete jump process.
  The default cap affects computational evaluation, not the number of
  observations, and can alter power/statistic values.
- Existence checks do not make Gaussian approximations uniformly accurate
  for rare-event designs. Even a legitimate known tiny Poisson mean can
  generate almost exclusively zero observations at practical sample sizes;
  empirical residual multipliers then poorly estimate the sampling variance.
  Large nominal n alone is not sufficient information. Severe imbalance,
  small expected counts and leverage need separate judgment or a justified
  family-specific calibration method, not an arbitrary universal cutoff.
- Report Monte Carlo resolution and precision. For an ideal conditional tail
  probability p, b/R has binomial simulation variance p(1-p)/R. A zero count
  is evidence of limited tail resolution, not an impossible null event.
  An exact randomized Monte Carlo p-value is a different claim; adding one
  does not remove the underlying asymptotic approximation of this method.
- Keep the scope of published methods honest: ordinary component checks
  are not the multivariate process in Lin's (2.5), and the SEM normality,
  missing-data weighting and GEE extensions require additional implementations.

* Mathematical review of proposed corrections (before final integration)

The independent source review of =R/mle-validity.R= found the nonlinear
score/Hessian formulas correct for binomial/log, Poisson/identity and sqrt,
and Gaussian/log and inverse. Gaussian curvature bounds include the required
interior stationary points, rather than relying on endpoint monotonicity
that does not hold. Direct original-design eta evaluation, scaled-design
comparison and explicit endpoint arithmetic allowances address the reviewed
normalization issue. This is a numerical Krawczyk-style local root qualification,
not a formal outward-rounded interval proof, proof of global optimality or
proof that an arbitrarily inaccurate fitted centre equals that root.

The proposed ordinal separation direction also has a sufficient proof.
For each observed category j, require nonnegative upper-bound movement
(delta alpha_j-X_i delta beta) and nonpositive lower-bound movement
(delta alpha_(j-1)-X_i delta beta), omitting infinite boundaries. A strict
movement for at least one observation makes every category-probability
likelihood contribution nondecreasing and at least one strictly increasing
when the CDF density is positive at its legal finite boundaries. Thus no
finite interior zero-score solution exists in that direction. If every
category is represented, adjacent threshold-gap directions are nonnegative
and preserve ordering. An empty estimated category instead requires a
cutpoint-gap/extreme-threshold boundary. The converse existence assertion
has not been proved here, and numerical LP infeasibility must not be described
as a universal global-MLE theorem for arbitrary ordinal links.

The proposed joint ordinal norm follows from the continuous mapping theorem
as described in F4. The proposed complete window-event grid follows directly
from the indicator endpoints in F2. Final integration must verify these
properties on the delivered source; this mathematical review does not
substitute for that implementation check.

* Bounded SEM constant-order check

A final two-seed check of the correlated-error SEM with an empty safe
conditioning set did not reproduce a new rejection error. Seeds 6080 and
911 give Sup/L2 p-values 1/1, with observed residual totals about 1e-15 and
numerical null totals about 1e-12 to 1e-10. The fixed-mean control deliberately
retains an overall-mean discrepancy and gives Sup=0, which is meaningful.
Evidence is =sem-constant.R= and =sem-constant.log=.

There is a sufficient mean-score no-information certificate. Write
V=L nu+L epsilon and use the conditional structural residual
r_t=e_t' P L' J' Sigma^-1(V_observed-mu). If free pure-intercept directions
can span P e_t without changing regression or covariance parameters, a
linear combination of fitted Gaussian mean scores is sum_i r_t. It is then
constrained to zero. A stronger easily checked structural condition is that
the pure intercept derivative basis spans every unit direction e_j allowed
by the structural pattern P[target,j]. The S08 example has both mean
intercepts free and meets that condition. A fixed mean need not meet it.

Such a certificate must refer to a named structural residual and the actual
fitted parameters/data. It must not silently erase a custom residual, an
explicit alternate parameter evaluation or a fixed-mean test merely because
the ordering is constant. Since neither bounded SEM probe produced a false
rejection, this observation is recorded as a no-information interpretation/
robustness consideration rather than an additional confirmed failing path.

** Acceptance statement

No reproduced candidate failure remains in this bounded final replay.
The concrete code defects identified by this review have repairs with
independent counterexample evidence; the callback and cross-variable
limitations are now explicit contracts/scope. The literature comparison,
source derivations and seeded probes support these stated endpoints and
assumptions. They do not establish that the package has no possible bugs,
that every arbitrary user extension is valid, or that a human statistician
has approved it.

The general limitations above still apply: regular independent units,
correct influence normalization, sufficient event information, valid
conditional centering, component versus global inference, and asymptotic
rather than exact finite-sample Monte Carlo calibration. In particular,
a displayed zero Monte Carlo tail fraction has finite simulation
resolution; it does not denote an impossible null event. These are
scientific use conditions rather than C++ modernization tasks.
